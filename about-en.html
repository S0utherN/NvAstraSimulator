What is this?

A night-vision astronomy eyepiece simulator that runs in the browser. Public survey data describe the nebulae and stars, and a physical model of the image intensifier (photocathode, microchannel plate, phosphor screen) simulates every photon event on the GPU in real time, showing roughly what you would see in the eyepiece with different tubes, filters and skies.

This site is free and non-commercial, for science outreach and research, with no ads and no paid content.

Privacy: no data collected

  • No analytics, ads, accounts or cookies. The page contains no analytics or tracking code and sends nothing you do, no parameters and no device information to any server.
  • Parameters live only in the link. Shared links encode the simulation parameters after #s=…; browsers never send the part after # to the server, so only people you share the link with can see it.
  • Stored on this device. The browser's local storage keeps only interface preferences — whether you have seen the tour, the quality and language choices, collapsed sections — and an error log (error message, version and page path). To open faster next time, data files you have viewed and the page itself are also cached on this device (Cache API and Service Worker); they are identical to the public files on the server. None of this is uploaded; clear it in the simulator under Settings → This device, or by clearing the browser's site data.
  • The error log stays on this device. If something goes wrong you can copy it from Settings → This device → Error log and decide yourself whether to send it to the author. The copied text includes the browser version and screen size, and you can see all of it before sending.
  • Hosting. The page and data files are served by a static host (currently GitHub Pages, possibly through a CDN such as Cloudflare), which may keep ordinary access logs (such as IP addresses) under its own policy; this site does not read or use them.
  • If any visitor statistics are ever added, this page and the simulator will say so first, and they will only be enabled with your consent.

Disclaimer: a simulation, not the real thing

  • The view, brightness, noise and “equivalent datasheet” are model results meant to explain how image intensifiers work and compare. They do not represent what any brand, model or individual tube actually shows or measures. Preset names are only parameter sets, not general product specifications.
  • The model relies on public survey data, numbers from papers and a number of assumptions (for example some targets lack measured [O III] or [S II] maps, and some cathode and phosphor curves are estimates); these gaps and assumptions are marked in the “?” notes throughout the simulator. What you actually see also depends on the telescope, the weather and your own eyes, and may differ noticeably from the simulation.
  • Do not use the results as the basis for purchases, trading, safety or any professional purpose. Buy and use night-vision equipment in accordance with local laws.
  • This site is provided “as is”, without liability for any consequences of using it or relying on its results.

Data sources and licences

Only processed derivatives are published here (resampled, converted units, encoded); no original FITS files, paper texts or images. Each data set is credited as its source requires; some of them may not be used commercially, which is one reason this site stays non-commercial.

Version 0.9.43 adds regional Cygnus line-map detail, the Heart/Soul Nebulae and Perseus Double Cluster, using NSNS and Gaia/Hipparcos under the acknowledgements and non-commercial terms below.

Version 0.9.45 adds ten deep-sky targets (NSNS), the Cepheus–Cassiopeia and Perseus–Auriga wide fields (NSNS with Finkbeiner), the Large Magellanic Cloud (SHASSA) and Butterfly (IC 1318) detail, under the same sources and terms.
